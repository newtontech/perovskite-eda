ABSTRACT: Self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) have emerged as a new generation of hole
transport materials (HTMs) for perovskite solar cells (PSCs), particularly in inverted
architectures. Compared to conventional HTMs, SAMs demonstrate superior power
conversion efficiency (PCE) and enhanced operational stability. However, the current
discovery of SAMs still relies heavily on empirical trial-and-error approaches, suffering from
long development cycles, high costs, and low success rates. Here we present a novel machine
learning (ML) platform for accelerated SAM discovery and design. We constructed a
comprehensive feature space combining RDKit molecular descriptors and Morgan
fingerprints, and then systematically evaluated various ML algorithms. Multiple evaluation
metrics were used to assess model reliability. The results demonstrate that the RDKit-based
XGBoost model achieved optimal performance with a root-mean-square error (RMSE) of
1.862, a coefficient of determination (R2) of 0.5058, a Pearson correlation coefficient (r) of
0.8161 and a mean absolute error (MAE) of 1.528. Then, SHapley Additive exPlanations
(SHAP) analysis further elucidated the structure−property relationships between key molecular features and photovoltaic
performance. The SHAP values revealed that the top five most important features were all RDKit descriptors, specifically
EState_VSA5, fr_benzene, EState_VSA2, SlogP_VSA1, and Chi0v. The external validation using recently reported SAM molecules
demonstrated remarkable prediction accuracy. The relative errors between predicted and experimental PCE values were mostly
within 10%, with the minimum being only 0.55%. Meanwhile, three new SAM molecules were designed based on the model, with
the highest predicted PCE approaching 27%. Therefore, this work provides an efficient digital solution for SAM development,
offering valuable guidance for accelerating the discovery of next-generation photovoltaic materials.
P
erovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted significant
attention as a third-generation photovoltaic technology
due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and low
manufacturing cost.1−4 Recent advances have pushed the PCE
of PSCs beyond 27%.5,6 Among interfacial materials, self-
assembled monolayers (SAMs) have shown great potential as
hole transport layers, especially in inverted architectures, owing
to their minimal parasitic absorption, low material usage, and
simple processing, which support the fabrication of large-area
and stable devices.7 However, current SAM design still relies
on conventional synthesis methods, such as reactions with
conjugated aldehydes, pyridines, or amine derivatives.6 These
approaches are not only time- and resource-intensive but also
offer no guarantee of achieving high PCE. Thus, there remains
a challenge to rapidly design and optimize SAMs for efficient
perovskite solar cells.
In PSCs, SAMs are ultrathin, ordered molecular layers that
self-assemble on substrates.6 A typical SAM comprises three
parts: an anchoring group, a spacer, and a functional group.7
The anchoring group bonds chemically to the substrate,
enabling film formation.8 The spacer connects the anchor to
the functional group,7 which interfaces with the perovskite,
enhances charge extraction, and modulates the substrate work
function via its molecular dipole.9,10 Recently, many studies
have exploited SAMs in PSCs. For example, Zhang et al.11 used
SAMs with different anchors and spacers to tune perovskite
interface properties in mixed Sn−Pb devices. Li et al.12 applied
Me-4PACz to stabilize the perovskite structure and passivate
interfaces. Zhou et al.13 synthesized benzothiadiazole-based
SAMs with varied anchoring groups for interface modification.
Despite these advances, SAM preparation remains largely trial-
and-error, with no assurance of high device performance.
Therefore, a method to rapidly screen high-PCE SAMs is
urgently needed.
In recent years, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI)
and materials science has opened new avenues for accelerated
material discovery. Machine learning (ML), a cornerstone of
AI, has demonstrated remarkable capability in establishing
quantitative structure−property relationships (QSPRs) and
predicting key material characteristics.14 Within perovskite

In this work, we present a data-driven ML framework
specifically tailored for the accelerated discovery and design of
high-performance SAMs for inverted PSCs. We constructed a
curated data set of SAM-based devices and systematically
evaluated multiple ML algorithms combined with different
molecular representations (RDKit descriptors and Morgan
fingerprints). The optimal model was identified and rigorously
interpreted using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to
elucidate critical structure−property relationships. The model’s
predictive reliability was further validated against newly
reported SAM molecules. Finally, we demonstrate its practical
utility by designing new SAM candidates with predicted PCEs
approaching 27%. This study provides an efficient computa-
tional strategy to guide the experimental development of next-
generation SAM-based photovoltaic devices.

To accurately model the relationship between the molecular
structure of SAM materials and PCE, appropriate input
features must be selected. We obtained the SMILES
(Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System) representa-
tions of all SAM molecules and converted them into Morgan
fingerprints and RDKit descriptors using Python and the web-
based tool http://www.scbdd.com/chemdes/. These were
used as input features for the machine learning models.
Morgan fingerprints encode the presence or absence of
molecular substructures in a binary format, where “1” indicates
the presence of a feature and “0” its absence. As a type of
circular fingerprint, Morgan fingerprints capture localized
structural fragments in a flexible and detailed manner, with
adjustable lengths to balance information richness and
computational efficiency.26 In this study, we tested Morgan
fingerprints with hash sizes of 256, 512, and 1024 to evaluate
their impact on model performance. Molecular descriptors, in
contrast, represent molecular properties using real-valued
numbers rather than structural encoding. They quantify
physicochemical and topological characteristics (e.g., atom
types, bond connectivity, etc.) to provide a numerical
representation of molecular features.27 In addition to the
SAM-derived features, we incorporated 26 additional input
parameters related to device fabrication and composition.
These parameters can be broadly categorized into: (1)
perovskite composition (e.g., types of cations and halides),
(2) solvent and additive information (e.g., solvent type,
concentration), (3) electron transport layer components, and
(4) processing conditions (e.g., whether NiO2 is used, whether
cleaning is performed). A complete list of these 26 features,
including their descriptions and categories, is provided in
Table S3. Categorical variables (e.g., presence/absence of a
specific component) were encoded using one-hot encoding to
ensure they could be properly handled by the machine learning
models. The RDKit descriptors-based final feature set for
model training consisted of 222 features: 196 RDKit molecular
descriptors and 26 device-specific parameters (Table S3).
While this represents a high-dimensional space relative to our
data set of 99 samples, we note that RDKit descriptors are
curated chemical informatics features with well-defined
physicochemical interpretations. Therefore, the RDKit de-
scriptors were used directly without dimensionality reduction
or explicit feature selection. To address potential overfitting
concerns inherent in such a scenario, we relied on the built-in
regularization capabilities of our chosen algorithms and
rigorous validation protocols. By analyzing these features, we
aim to elucidate their influence-both qualitatively and
quantitatively on the PCE of SAM-based inverted perovskite
solar cells. This approach will provide actionable insights to
guide the experimental synthesis of SAM materials and the
optimization of device fabrication processes.

RF is a bagging-based ensemble learning method that
enhances model robustness by constructing multiple inde-
pendent decision trees and combining their predictions.
During training, each tree is trained on a randomly sampled
subset of data (with replacement) and considers only a random
selection of features at each node split to promote model
diversity. The final prediction is determined through voting
(for classification) or averaging (for regression), significantly
reducing overfitting risks. Known for its simplicity, paralleliz-
ability, and noise resistance, RF is widely applied to high-
dimensional data and nonlinear problems.
GBDT is a boosting-based ensemble algorithm that
sequentially trains decision trees to correct errors from
previous iterations. GBDT adopts a stepwise training strategy
where each new tree fits the negative gradient (pseudor-
esiduals) of the current model, progressively refining
predictions. The algorithm supports various loss functions
and balances bias-variance trade-offs by adjusting tree depth
and learning rate.
We then divided the entire data set into 80% for training and
20% for testing. Given the limited number of unique SAM
molecules (over 50) and the fact that some molecules appear
in multiple data points (due to variations in device fabrication
or measurement conditions), we adopted a point-level splitting
strategy. This means that data points from the same SAM
molecule could appear in both the training and test sets, which
reflects real-world scenarios where the same material may be
tested under different conditions. While this approach carries a
risk of data leakage, it allows us to utilize the full data set for
training and evaluation. To mitigate this risk, we employed
rigorous cross-validation and external validation on entirely
unseen SAM molecules. Given the feature-to-sample ratio,
particular attention was paid to mitigating overfitting. The tree-
based models were trained with their respective regularization
mechanisms enabled. For XGBoost, this includes L1
(reg_alpha) and L2 (reg_lambda) regularization terms within
its objective function. Hyperparameter optimization via
GridSearchCV explicitly tuned these parameters alongside
others controlling tree depth (max_depth) and learning rate
(learning_rate), ensuring a balance between model complexity
and generalization ability. Subsequently training three ML
algorithms (XGBoost, RF, and GBDT) using both RDKit
descriptors and Morgan fingerprints of different lengths (256,
512, and 1024 bits) as input features, resulting in a total of 12
trained models. For each model, we optimized the hyper-
parameters using GridSearchCV and evaluated their perform-
ance based on four metrics: RMSE, R2, r and MAE, to ensure
the highest possible model accuracy. Meanwhile, during the
model construction process, standardization was applied to the
features to eliminate the influence of unit and scale differences
among variables and to ensure each feature dimension is

After identifying the optimal model, we conducted model
interpretation to examine the influence of individual features
on the predictive outcomes and understand how these features
affect the model’s performance. Machine learning models often
function as “black boxes”while we can observe their output
predictions, the underlying relationships between input
features and model results remain obscure. Traditional feature
importance methods can only indicate which features are more
significant but fail to reveal their specific impact on model
outputs. To address this limitation, we employed SHapley
Additive exPlanations (SHAP), an interpretability framework
rooted in game theory that provides comprehensive explan-
ations for machine learning model outputs.29 The core of this
approach lies in Shapley values, a concept originally developed
by Lloyd Shapley in 1953 to fairly distribute payoffs among
participants in cooperative games.30 SHAP quantifies each
feature’s contribution to model predictions by calculating its
marginal impact across all possible feature combinations,
ensuring equitable attribution of influence among all variables.
This method offers dual capabilities: it enables both local
interpretation of individual predictions and global model
interpretation through aggregation of multiple Shapley
values.31

Figure 3 displays the performance results (RMSE, MAE, R2,
and r) for each model. The heatmaps reveal that XGBoost with
RDKit achieved the lowest RMSE (1.862), followed by
Morrgan_1024-based GBDT (1.876). For R2, XGBoost and
GBDT significantly outperformed RF, with RDKit-based
XGBoost showing the highest R2 (0.5058). Similarly, for the
r metric, RF models consistently underperformed compared to
XGBoost and GBDT, with the best XGBoost model (RDKit

(Morgan_1024 input) reaching r of 0.7412. In terms of MAE,
the performance rankings of the three models were distinct.
The GBDT model achieved the lowest MAE values across
most feature sets, followed by the RF model, while the
XGBoost model consistently exhibited the highest MAE.
Overall, the RDKit-based XGBoost model demonstrated
superior performance with RMSE of 1.862, R2 of 0.5058, r
of 0.8161 and MAE of 1.528, while RF models performed
significantly worse than both XGBoost and GBDT. The
complete performance metrics for all models are summarized
in Table 1. Meanwhile, Figure S5 further presents the
performance metrics of the 12 models after 10-fold cross-
validation. Collectively, the RDKit-based XGBoost model
performed the best.
Figure 4 presents the SHAP interpretation results for the
optimal model in this study-the RDKit-based XGBoost model
(Figures S2−S12 display the SHAP interpretation results for
the other models). Figure 4a shows the importance ranking of
each feature in the model, where the vertical axis represents
each feature (ranked from top to bottom, with higher positions
indicating greater importance to the model results). The
horizontal axis shows the mean absolute value of Shapley
values for each feature, representing their average influence on
the model output. From the ranking, we can see that the top 5
features are all RDKit descriptors, namely EState_VSA5,
fr_benzene, EState_VSA2, SlogP_VSA1 and Chi0v, which
have a significant impact on the model output, and the
combined influence of these five features far exceeds that of
other features. This indicates that changes in the model’s
output are largely affected by these few features. Furthermore,
among the top 10 important features, nearly all are RDKit
descriptors, suggesting that molecular descriptors play a
dominant role in determining the photovoltaic performance
prediction in our model. Figure 4b further explains how each
feature influences the model output by analyzing their positive
or negative contributions to the predictions using the SHAP
additive explanation algorithm. In this result plot, each row
represents a feature, and each dot represents a data point. The
features are again ranked in descending order of their
importance to the model output. The horizontal axis displays
both positive and negative SHAP values, with red portions
indicating high feature values and blue portions indicating low
feature values. The results show that for the top-ranked feature
EState_VSA5, high values (red) cluster in the negative SHAP
region, indicating a detrimental effect on PCE, while low values
(blue) exert a positive effect. For EState_VSA2, the SHAP
result shows an opposite trend. It can be observed from the
SHAP analysis that when its feature value is low, it exerts a
negative contribution to the model’s predicted PCE outcome,
whereas a higher feature value results in a positive
contribution. The relationships for Chi0v and SlogP_VSA1
are more complex. For Chi0v, an intermediate range of values
yields a positive contribution to the model’s output, while
values that are either too low or too high exhibit a negative
impact. Similarly, SlogP_VSA1, shows low feature values
contributing negatively and high values contributing positively
in SHAP analysis, suggesting that an appropriate level of
hydrophobicity is beneficial for enhancing PCE.
Expanding the physical interpretation, these SHAP-derived
trends can be linked to plausible SAM-perovskite interface
mechanisms. EState_VSA5, quantifying specific atomic electro-
topological contributions to molecular surface area,32 corre-
lates negatively with PCE when high. This suggests that
excessive localized polarity may disrupt optimal interfacial
dipole formation and energy level alignment, which iscritical
for efficient charge extraction. SlogP_VSA1, a descriptor of
hydrophobic surface area,33 shows a positive correlation
beyond a threshold. Adequate hydrophobicity likely enhances
interfacial moisture resistance and promotes denser SAM
packing, both vital for device stability and uniform charge
transport. Chi0v, a valence connectivity index related to
molecular size and complexity,32 exhibits an optimal
intermediate range for PCE. This implies a balance is needed:
sufficient molecular size for effective interface modification, yet
avoiding excessive bulk that could hinder monolayer order or
introduce defects. While these descriptor-property links offer
valuable design insights, they represent statistical correlations
whose precise atomic-scale mechanisms warrant further
experimental validation.

feature Chi0v, its contribution to the model is positive when its
value lies within the 15−25 range. This positive impact is
maximized at values around 20. Conversely, values outside this
range have a negative impact on the model’s performance.
Through this SHAP interpretation of the model results, we
have further demonstrated the degree of influence each feature
has on the PCE output of our optimal model and how they
affect the model’s predictions.
The interaction plot can reveal the marginal effects of one or
two features on a machine learning model’s prediction,
illustrating both the relationships between features and the
target variable, and the interactions between features
themselves. To further elucidate the pairwise interactions
between different descriptor features and their impact on the
model output, we analyzed the SHAP interaction values for
EState_VSA5, EState_VSA2, SlogP_VSA1, and Chi0v, as
shown in Figure 6. Figures 6a and 6b present the interaction
effects on the model output when EState_VSA5 is considered
as the main feature, paired with SlogP_VSA1 and EState_V-
SA2, respectively. From Figure 6a, it can be observed that a
positive contribution to the PCE is achieved only when both
EState_VSA5 and SlogP_VSA1 exhibit low values. When these
two features take on medium or high values, their joint effect
on the model output becomes negative. Conversely, Figures 6c
and 6d display the interaction effects with SlogP_VSA1 as the
main feature, paired with EState_VSA5 and Chi0v, respec-
tively. From the results in Figure 6c, it can be seen that when
SlogP_VSA1 takes a small value, the feature value of
EState_VSA5 also needs to be small to have a positive
contribution to the model. In contrast, the interaction between
Chi0v and SlogP_VSA1 shows the opposite trend: as shown in
Figure 6d, when SlogP_VSA1 takes a small value, the feature
Chi0v needs to take a larger value to have a positive
contribution to the model.
After completing the training of all models and identifying
the best-performing model, the RDKit-based XGBoost model,
we further validated its reliability and explored its application
in screening and designing high-PCE SAM materials. We
collected several newly synthesized SAM molecules reported in
2025 literature and input them into our optimal model for
PCE prediction. The results are shown in Figure 7. We
selected nine SAM molecules with reported PCEs between
22% and 27% for testing. Figure S13 shows the molecular
structures of these SAM molecules. As shown in Figure 7, the
PCE values predicted by the model for most molecules align
closely with their actual reported PCEs. Through calculation,
the relative errors are largely within 10%, with a minimum
error of only 0.55%. Table 2 summarizes the experimental
PCEs, model-predicted PCEs, and relative errors for these
molecules. These results further demonstrate the reliability of
our trained model in predicting PCE for SAM-based inverted
perovskite solar cells. Moreover, this work provides valuable
guidance and strategies for machine learning-assisted screening
and design of high-PCE SAM materials.
Then, with the guidance of our model, three new SAM
molecular structures were designed and designated as SAM1,
SAM2 and SAM3, respectively. Their molecular structures are
shown in Figure 8. Based on our previous SHAP analysis, the
most important features influencing the model output are
descriptors such as EState_VSA5. The EState_VSA5 values for
the three newly designed SAM molecules are 0, 6.42 and 0,
which not only falls within the optimal range of 0−50 as
identified by the SHAP analysis but also corresponds to the
condition that maximizes the feature’s positive contribution to
the predicted PCE; their EState_VSA2 values are 12.58, 12.58
and 17.61; Chi0v values are 17.93, 18.23 and 18.76,
respectively. It can be observed that the values of these key
descriptors for the SAM molecules all lie within the expected
ranges indicated by the SHAP analysis. Subsequently, based on
the SHAP results, appropriate values were also assigned to
other features such as perovskite composition to achieve a
predicted high PCE outcome. The predicted PCE values for
these three SAM molecules are 26.94%, 26.73%, and 26.60%,
respectively, suggesting their promising theoretical perform-
ance. These results demonstrate that our model can effectively
assist in the design and screening of high-performance SAM
candidates for perovskite solar cells, thereby potentially
accelerating the development of SAM-based photovoltaic
devices. It should be noted, however, that these predictions
await experimental validation.
In this study, we compiled a data set of 99 self-assembled
monolayer materials from published literature on inverted
perovskite solar cells. We systematically constructed molecular
representations using RDKit descriptors and three variants of
Morgan fingerprints, and trained a total of 12 machine learning
models by combining these features with three distinct
algorithms to predict the PCE of SAM-based inverted
perovskite solar cells. Our results demonstrate that the
XGBoost model utilizing RDKit descriptors achieved optimal
performance, with r of 0.8161, RMSE of 1.862, R2 of 0.5058
and MAE of 1.528. The SHAP analysis revealed that the top
five features most significantly influencing the model’s output
results were all RDKit descriptors generated from SAM
molecules, specifically EState_VSA5, fr_benzene, EState_V-
SA2, SlogP_VSA1, and Chi0v. These critical descriptors
provide valuable insights for rational SAM design and
screening. External validation using newly synthesized SAM
materials confirmed the model’s reliability. The relative errors
between the predicted and experimental PCE values were
mostly within 10%, with a minimum error of only 0.55%.
Moreover, three new SAM molecules designed under the
guidance of our model with the highest predicted PCE
approaching 27%, demonstrating its significant application
potential. This work establishes an efficient machine learning
framework for accelerated self-assembled monolayer materials
discovery and offers valuable guidance for developing high-
performance organic photovoltaic materials and devices. It is
worth noting that the current model is built upon a data set of
99 SAM-based PSC devices. While this data set reflects some
high-quality experimental data currently reported in the field,
the performance and generalizability of machine learning
models can typically be enhanced by larger and more
structurally diverse data sets. Future efforts to expand this
database, for instance, by including newly published SAM
structures and additional experimental parameters, will be
